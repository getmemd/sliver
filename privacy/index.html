Privacy Policy

Effective November 14, 2026

In short:

  • Sliver runs on your device. There is no account, we run no servers, and the app itself sends nothing over the internet.
  • Your photos never leave your device. Photos are deleted only after you confirm in the iOS system dialog.
  • We can't see which apps you use or how much time you spend in them.
  • The app has no ads, analytics, tracking, or third-party SDKs.

This policy explains what data the Sliver app for iPhone and iPad handles, where that data is stored, and what you can do with it. “We” means the developer of Sliver. You can reach us at the address under Contact.

What we don't collect

The app doesn't send data to us or to anyone else. It makes no network requests to our servers or anyone else's. It has no sign-up, no ads, no analytics or tracking tools, and no third-party data-collection libraries. We never receive your photos, information about your apps, or your review statistics.

Photos

  • Why access is needed. Sliver needs full access to your photo library to show you photos to review and to delete the ones you send to the trash.
  • Photos stay on your device. The app displays your photos on screen and never uploads or copies them anywhere. Review categories such as screenshots, bursts of similar shots, and videos are based on metadata iOS provides: capture date, media type, and burst membership. The contents of your photos are never sent anywhere.
  • iCloud Photos. If your photos are stored in iCloud, iOS may download them so they can be shown on screen. Apple's system does this under iCloud's terms. Our servers aren't involved.
  • Deletion. Photos are removed from your library only after you confirm the deletion in the iOS system dialog. Deleted photos go to the Recently Deleted album, where the Photos app handles them according to iOS rules.
  • Review log. To avoid showing you the same photo twice and to keep your stats, the app stores the following on your device:
    • identifiers of reviewed photos (iOS's internal photo library identifiers, not the photos themselves), your decision (keep or trash), and when you made it;
    • the queue of photos waiting to be deleted, with their approximate sizes;
    • daily totals: photos reviewed, kept, and trashed, approximate space freed, daily goals completed, and emergency access used.
    The log is kept in the app's own storage. Sliver's extensions don't read it, and it doesn't sync with iCloud. On the “All reviewed” screen you can choose to go through your photos again. Doing so erases the list of reviewed photos but keeps your stats.

Screen Time and the apps you choose

  • Why access is needed. You grant Screen Time access for your own device. Sliver uses it to block the apps you choose until you complete your daily goal.
  • You choose in Apple's picker. You pick apps, categories, and websites in an iOS system screen. Sliver receives only opaque tokens. iOS uses them to know what to block, but Sliver can't tell what you selected. App names and icons on screen are drawn by the system.
  • We don't see your usage. Your Screen Time data stays with iOS. Sliver receives only service signals from the system, such as a new day starting or total time in your chosen apps reaching a set limit (for example, when emergency access time runs out). These signals contain no per-app breakdown. An approximate count of those minutes is stored on your device along with the rest of the app's state.
  • What is stored. A shared container used by the app and its extensions (an App Group) holds:
    • your selection of apps, categories, and websites, as tokens;
    • your settings and today's status: goal size, mode, photos reviewed today, whether the goal is done, emergency access used and when the current one ends, and the approximate minute count;
    • your chosen theme.
    During first-time setup, your app selection is temporarily kept in the app's own settings. The block screen only reads this state to show how many photos are left until your goal.

Notifications

All of Sliver's notifications are local. The app creates them on your device, and we have no push server. Sliver sends a reminder about your daily goal if it isn't done yet, and a notification that opens Sliver when you tap a button on the block screen. You can turn reminders off in Sliver's settings, and all notifications off in iOS Settings.

Purchases

In-app purchases (Sliver Pro, a one-time purchase) go through the App Store. Apple processes the payment, and we never receive your payment details or your Apple ID. iOS stores and verifies your purchase. Payment processing is covered by Apple's Privacy Policy.

What Apple may receive

  • Backups. If you back up to iCloud or a computer, the app's data may be included along with the rest of your device's data, depending on your iOS settings.
  • Reports for developers. If “Share With App Developers” is turned on in iOS Settings (Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements), Apple may share anonymized crash reports and general app usage statistics with us. This is an iOS setting, and you can turn it off at any time.
  • App Store. Apple gives developers aggregate download and sales statistics. We can't see individual users in them.

If you email us

If you contact support, we receive your email address and anything you include in your message. We use this only to reply to you and don't share it with anyone.

Retention and deletion

  • All of Sliver's data stays on your device until you delete the app. When you do, iOS erases the data along with the app.
  • Data included in a backup stays there until you delete that backup or it is overwritten.
  • Photos you keep are left unchanged. Deleted photos stay in Recently Deleted under the Photos app's rules.
  • Apart from any emails you send us, we hold none of your data, so there is nothing for us to export, correct, or delete on our side. It's all under your control on your device. You can ask us to delete our correspondence with you.

Children

Sliver is not directed at children. The app doesn't collect data about anyone, including children. Sliver requests Screen Time access for your own device, not to manage a child's device.

Changes

If this policy changes, we'll update this page and the effective date at the top. If the app starts handling data differently, for example sending anything off your device, we'll describe the change here before the version that includes it is released.
